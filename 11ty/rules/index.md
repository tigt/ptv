# _Pokémon: Tabletop Version_ Core Rules

## Differences from other Pokémon tabletop systems

There are 3 unavoidable issues with Pokémon tabletops:

1. Compromise: 100% video game faithfulness makes bad tabletops, 100% tabletop conventions make a bad Pokémon game.
2. Complicated: there's how many Pokémon, again?
3. Unbalanceable: not even infinite playtesting is good enough across unique playgroups and campaign metagames.

PTV tackles these issues by **making things as easy as we can for Game Masters** to reason about. The best way to do that is to remove things. The next is to not add things. Lastly, try other tricks to manage complexity:

Reuse existing knowledge
: Work like the video games first. Who’s playing that’s not a big ol’ Pokémon nerd?
: Rolling d20 against a DC isn’t perfect, but people already get it. Plus it’s _fast_, which leads to…

Fast-but-imperfect is better than smart-but-slow
: Choosing Moves, plotting with other players, and improvising are more fun than looking up rules and juggling numbers.
: Leaving brainspace open for things like exploration, intrigue, and campaign settings is usually more fun overall.
: Keep the main rules simple even if that means making specific rules more complex, to reduce what knowledge everyone must share.
: Faster turns encourage experimenting, risky memorable plays, and make wasted turns feel better.

New systems are intimidating
: Make early decisions easy. (For example, Classes start few and without prerequisites. Building level 1 Pokémon only requires base game info, with an optional Nature.)
: If we don’t welcome new players, we’ll have ***no*** players. Experienced players can start at higher levels.
: Introduce new concepts, Abilities, and complexity later if possible, once players have learned the basics.

- Skills vastly simplified. Your Pokémon can already handle a ton of interesting world interactions, and for everything else there’s Talents and Flaws.

## Intentional differences from the video games

- Additive instead of multiplicative math, so the numbers don’t match exactly but we try to make them feel about the same.

- **Pokémon can have a Bonus Move:** a 5<sup>th</sup> move slot filled by their Trainer’s Class(es).

- **AP replaced with AP**, which works as more of a spendable resource each character has instead of tracking usage of each Move. As a result, AP-related things like Spite, AP Ups, Pressure, etc. work differently.

- **Infatuation isn’t as annoying.**

- Ability changes:
  + **Pokémon gain a second Ability at Level 5.**
  + **New Abilities added** (many, but they don’t show up until Level 5).
  + **Lackluster Abilities get Bonus Stats** to make them more worth choosing.
  + **Abilities that care about certain Moves sometimes affect more Moves**:
    * **Wind Rider/Wind Power** also trigger for Razor Wind, Leaf Tornado, Leaf Storm, and Mysterious Air Currents. (Also Storm Throw due to a wording quirk, but that almost never matters.)
    * **Sharpness** also buffs Dual Chop, Razor Wind, and Precipice Blades.
    * Some additions only make sense in English: sorry EN/JP-bilingual players, but **Iron Fist**+Sucker Punch is a mistake *every single playtester* made. Also, Iron Fist buffs “Hammer” Moves, which oversteps only buffing Hammer Arm and Ice Hammer, but it was cool so we let it buff others like Dragon Hammer.
  + **Run Away** also now buffs switching in Trainer Battles. (Every playtester who didn’t battle competitively thought it did.)
  + **Toxic Boost** now works subtly differently than Guts, to emphasize Zangoose’s “thing” and to avoid a nonbo with its base Immunity Ability.
  + **Zen Mode** now lets you choose a second moveset when switching modes.

- Move changes:
  + **All once-canon Moves supported**, since keeping them in an imagination-powered tabletop requires way less effort than programming and animating them.
  + **Power Swap swaps Physical ***and*** Special stats**, like it originally did in *Legends: Arceus*.
  + Celebrate/Hold Hands tweaked to have at least a niche use.
  + **Moves that fail unless used by their signature user (like Dark Void) no longer do**, at GM discretion.
  + **Mirror Move is now a Sonic move**, mostly to combo with Punk Rock and other things like that. (It’s “Parrot Mimicry” in Japanese)
  + **New Moves added** (not many).

- Annoying evolution methods gained alternatives:
  + **_All_ Eeveelutions can evolve with evolutionary stones.**
  + **_All_ evolutions from strong magnetic fields can use Thunder Stones.**
  + **Trade evolutions can also evolve by level** or using up trade item.

- **Stone evolutions still learn moves by level-up.**

- **Levels compressed to 1–20 instead of 1–100.**

- **Statuses stack**: you can have Sleep/Poison/Burn/Paralysis/Frozen simultaneously.

- No Breeding rules (we don’t know how to make them fun, but you can try if you want). Eggs also no longer take up space in the party.

- Trainers can be attacked, and some Trainers can fight back.

- **Level-up movesets overhauled**:
  + No in-between dead levels without new Moves.
  + Makes some Pokémon usable without guaranteed TM/TR support.
  + Added strong Moves to weaker Pokémon to power them up.
  + Adds fun/interesting/fitting Moves to more Pokémon.
  + Jokes and references to Pokémon design origins.

- By default, **Pokémon level up with their Trainers**; no tracking EXP for each.

- **Some Egg Groups renamed** if their original names were confusable with Types:
  + Water 1 → Aquatic
  + Water 3 → Crustacean (Yeah, we know about Archen. Better names welcome)
  + Grass → Plant
  + Bug → Insect
  + Fairy → Pixie
  + etc.

## How does PTV differ from [Pokémon Odyssey](https://pokemontabletop.com) (formerly Pokémon Tabletop United)?

PO focuses on tabletop gameplay, with mechanics inspired by but not working like the Pokémon video games. If you want any of the following, you may prefer PO:

- Lots of crunchy grid combat
- Mostly new Moves and Abilities instead of conversions from the videogames
- Abstractions of travel, items, money, and other non-battle mechanics
