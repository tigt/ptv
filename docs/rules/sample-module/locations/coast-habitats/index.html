Coast habitats

Starting areas. Lots of randomly-generated encounters here, and a good place to find wild Pokémon.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seashore_wildlife https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_cleaning_machine https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast

These habitats are listed in relative order of danger.

Jettytown Fields

Initial farming efforts with raised beds and paddies, but kind of constrained with how big they can be by the sea and the forbidden interior of the island. Compost production only goes so fast.

Has their own apiary and flower fields, for pollination reasons.

Any berries you bring back here can be planted and harvested.

  • Encouraged species: Diglett, Smoliv, birds that eat bugs, spinarak, bees, cutiefly
  • Pests: herbivorous Bug-Types, Skwovet, Eldegoss, Crabrawler
  • Working species: Onix (plowing), Mudsdale, Simipour, Aggron

Seaglass Beach

Where debris from the tower’s exhaust strait reaches the sea. The Trash Corps clean up everything that’s an active hazard, leaving behind so much inert material the entire beach is made of seaglass, tiny particles, and shards of all kinds.

There’s big warning signs mandating protective equipment before entry, on account of sharp debris everywhere, and a list of Pokémon species known to be in the area. (This doesn’t mean you can’t find others, just that the Corps haven’t seen them yet.)

Out in the water are buoys, marking a large debris net.

You can go beachcombing for type gems, other items that resemble colored glass enough the trash corps might have missed it.

  • treasures: Beach Glass, Type Gems, evo stones, pearls, pearl strings, star pieces, comet shards, red/yellow/green/blue shards?, tropical shell, bottle caps?, oval stone, mystic water
  • You run the risk of fighting with wilds or getting caught by Trash Corps the longer you beachcomb. The Trash Corps may Trainer Battle you, then leave you alone if you win handily, which you probably will, since clearly you can handle any Pokémon nearby. They will still insist on you staying clearly visible so their equipment/cleaning Pokémon can’t hurt you, though.
  • Sharp glass or hazmats, which can take off some HP/status if you fail an Acrobatics check without gloves. (Seaglass can take a long time to become safe…)

Searching tends to be Focus checks to notice small things, and Endurance checks for thoroughness/to keep going. You may also use Stealth checks to search while avoiding attention. If you’re looking for something that someone intentionally hid, Intuition may help, since they probably used Deception to hide it. Don’t forget a light source if you’re searching at night.

Possible encounters:

  • On this beach, Sandygast and Staryu tend to be buried slightly so that battling them is a constant risk. Any battle also has a chance to begin with Spikes or Toxic Spikes set up.
  • Murkrow swarming around something especially shiny: Carbink. It’s terrified right now, but if you show up and start seriously taking on the Murkrow, it’ll help you fight them off. (Note this may get you into a Honchkrow’s bad graces, but that’s not a high bar and could be a fun callback/reason later for a battle.)
  • Murkrow attacking a Sableye, trying to get its gems
  • Meowth (incl. variants) squabbling over a shiny, but are all willing to attack you: they may not want each other to have it, but they all agree you definitely shouldn’t have it
  • Spoink looking for its lost pearl
  • Very rarely, a Gabite looking for things to hoard
  • Wimpod, beachcleaning for organic matter to eat. They are notorious cowards and flee if they see you coming.
  • Washed-up Pyukumuku, which you should probably throw back into the ocean. If you do, there’s a chance a passing Pelipper will swoop to catch it (like in New Snap), dropping something it was already carrying.
  • Trash Corps needing/waiting for help with certain things that you can do to get in their good books. Especially if you KO their Pokémon while they try to keep you out, they may admit that clearly you can handle yourself, but also now their working Pokémon are in no shape to work.

Tideplains

Only accessible when the tide goes out (roughly every 8 hours). At high tide, there’s not much to do other than fishing, swimming, or sitting on a thin strip of sand.

  • Krabby, Shellder, Pyukumuku, Clamperl, tentacool, frillish?, skrelp, dhelmise?, stunfisk, flamigo
  • treasure: Soft Sand, Pamtre Berries, Kelpsy Berries, Babiri Berries (do they grow near shorelines?), Ganlon Berries (sandy acidic soils in the tropics, I think so), Salac Berries (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salak), shoal salt, pretty feather, pearl, blue shard, tropical shell, heart scales, dragon scale, float stone
  • Tidepools: staryu, mareanie, clauncher, pincurchin, shellos, binacle, wimpod, clobbopus, fishing Slowbro, shellder, luvdisc, octillery?, dratini?
    • treasure: Hard Stone, Pamtre Berries, water stones?, shoal shells, pretty feather, blue shard, tropical shell, heart scales, water-egg-group Eggs, Damp Rock

Mangrove Forest

The most diverse habitat, which therefore has the most dangerous species. Traversal is also remarkably difficult, with mangrove roots snagging everything except small breaks of open water.

  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove_forest
  • barracuda, seahorses (finally!), mudskippers (mudkip), crabs, maybe some lobsters, surskit, monkeys, dewpider/araquanid, carvanha
